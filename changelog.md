# Changelog

All notable changes to Wavelength. Versions follow semantic versioning.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- GarageBand's Spreader (16 patches: Power Pad, Wide Suitcase, Massive Saws...) plays as a `chorus` whose two sides sweep in opposite directions, measured on GarageBand bounces of 1 kHz and 200 Hz sines: a delay swept from zero at half its Speed, Intensity setting the pitch wobble (+-16 cents at 50), Mix a crossfade. `chorus` gains `spread` (the right side's sweep that far into its cycle, 0.25 as before).
- GarageBand's Ringshifter (23 patches) and Pedalboard's Roswell Ringer play as `ringmod`: ring modulator, frequency shifter and dual modes, Frequency on its Exp (5000 Hz x 10^(5(x - 1))) or Lin (5000 Hz x) scale, Dry/Wet, Feedback, its delay, and its LFO sweeping the frequency (in octaves on the Exp scale). Every scale was measured on GarageBand bounces of a 1 kHz sine through test patches: frequencies land within 0.1 Hz and the feedback ladder within 1 dB. Ringshifter's side-chain mode and envelope follower, and Dry/Wet moving with its LFO, aren't played. `ringmod` gains `"freqScale": "exp"` (its curves and LFO in octaves).
- `tune` effect: pitch correction for a solo line. Its pitch is tracked every 5 ms and pulled to the nearest note of a `scale` (a key or a list of notes; every note by default), with a `tolerance` it leaves alone, a `response` time (0 snaps, the robotic step), `detune`, `reference` and `range`, formants kept. GarageBand's Pitch Correction plays as it (Alien Communication, Vintage Voice, Electric Sheep, Set to Zero Pitch, Change The Formant).
- `ringmod` effect: a ring modulator (`"mode": "ring"`, both sidebands) and frequency shifter (`"shift"`, one sideband, up or down; `"dual"`, the left side up and the right down) at `freq` Hz (automatable, LFO-able), with an echo of the effect (`delay`, `delayLevel`), `feedback` (a barber-pole spiral with a delay), `spread` and `mix`.
- `pitch` effect: moves the pitch by `semitones` and `cents` (automatable) and keeps the time, with the formants moving along or held (`keepFormants`) or moved on their own (`formant`), and a `mix`; the output lines up with the input. GarageBand's Vocal Transformer (9 voice patches: Change the Pitch, Deeper Vocal, Helium Vocal, Monster Vocal, Robot Harmony...) and Pitch Shifter (Glass Sky) play as it; Robotize, which pulls the melody toward one note, isn't played.
- builtin:synth tuned filters: `filter.type` takes `peak` (`gain`, `q`), `notch`, `formant` (a resonance, `q` times louder at its centre), `comb` (a feedback comb of the cutoff's period: `resonance` as its feedback, `"negative": true` for the hollow odd-harmonic kind, `damp`), `ring` (a ring modulator at the cutoff: `offset`, `weird`), `fm` (a sine at the cutoff whose phase the voice moves: `depth`, `feedback`) and `downsample` (held at twice the cutoff), each following the note with `keytrack` as the low-pass does. `filter` can be a list of up to 4 filters in series, `"parallel": true` adding one's output beside the one before it (a bank of formants), each with a `mix`; an oscillator's `"filter": 1` joins the list at that filter, and an LFO's `"filter": 1` moves that one's cutoff.
- Track `"panLaw": "balance"`: the side a track is panned to stays at its level and the other falls as (1 - |pan|)², the way GarageBand and Logic pan a stereo track (half left takes the right 12 dB down; the default constant-power law, 7.7 dB). Measured on a GarageBand bounce; imported GarageBand projects use it.
- GarageBand's guitar and bass amps play as built-in effects: Amp Designer (each amp model's gain stage, tone stack, tremolo or vibrato, spring reverb, power amp, presence, and its cabinet and microphone as EQ curves), Bass Amp Designer (amp, cabinet, D.I. blend, compressor, graphic or parametric EQ) and Pedalboard (its chain of stompboxes: drives and fuzzes, wahs, choruses, phasers, flangers, tremolo, rotary, delays, compressor, EQ; a frequency split as `multiband`), decoded from the 254 records in GarageBand's 161 patches that use them and Apple's presets. Patches and imported GarageBand projects (a guitar track's channel strip) get them. Each stage is level-matched so Output and Level set the loudness; ring modulation, the octaver, Wham and reverse delays are left out with a note, and the gain laws and cabinet curves are approximations.
- Retro Synth's Table mode plays its patch's own wavetable: a patch on a wavetable carries the table in its settings (harmonic amplitudes per wave, a binary property list), and each oscillator plays the wave at its Shape position as an additive oscillator, named from GarageBand's own table list at run time (Synth Chime Wash, Bright Digital Chords, Chordal Chimes, Glass Gliss; the others use Retro Synth's built-in waves, which no data file holds, and keep their saws). A sweep through the table (LFO or envelope) and the formant stretch are not played yet.
- Track `midiFx`: a track's notes through a chain of MIDI effects, in order, on any instrument, modelled on Logic's and GarageBand's: `chord` (Chord Trigger: a chord on every note, or one chord per key with the other keys silent, over a key range), `transpose` (Transposer: semitones, then onto the nearest note of a scale, given as a key such as `"A minor"` or a list of notes), `repeat` (Note Repeater: repeats a note value apart, each a number of semitones from the last and at a velocity ramp, the original or not) and `arp` (the arpeggiator; a track's `"arp"` stays as its shorthand). GarageBand's patches now play their whole MIDI chain by themselves, in slot order: Classic Chords, Chime Chords and Stutter Stack play their chords (Classic Chords then an octave down), Screamlead an octave up, and Funk Bot its arpeggio and its repeats; `"midiFx": false` plays the notes as written. The settings of the Chord Trigger (its Single and Multi chord maps, and the older layout two patches keep), Transposer and Note Repeater were decoded from GarageBand's channel strips and the effects' presets; any track can borrow one of their 66 presets, or a patch's, by name (`{"type": "chord", "preset": "Single/Triads/Major"}`), and `wavelength presets chord`, `presets transpose` and `presets repeat` list them. The engine plays the same notes as the decoder's reference on every patch and preset that uses these effects (Funk Bot's arpeggio is random; its repeats match). `samples --patch` shows a patch's chain as `midiFx`. A pass-through Scripter and GarageBand's built-in instrument scripts play the notes as written without a warning; Blue Ridge's Chord Trigger, left at its default chord on a drum kit, is skipped with one. New example `examples/midifx-tour.json`.
- `wavelength import song.band`: GarageBand projects (GarageBand 10 for Mac) become jobs, read by a new reader of GarageBand's ProjectData (its chunk container, tracks, channel strips, regions and events, decoded from project files alone) and MetaData.plist (binary or XML) that runs on any system. Each track keeps its channel strip as its own patch folder (`patches/<track>.patch`), so it plays the way GarageBand's patches play here: Sampler, EXS24 and Drum Kit Designer instruments on builtin:sampler, Apple's synths re-created on builtin:synth, a Sampler instrument made in the project from its Media folder, and a built-in `fallback` for an instrument that doesn't play. Faders (exact, on GarageBand's 40 log10(v/90) dB law, checked against faders set to 0, -6, -12, -30 and +3 dB), pans, mute and solo (a solo mutes the other tracks, as GarageBand plays it), sends (to buses named after GarageBand's aux, such as "Reverb", carrying its effects; GarageBand's Echo as a tempo-synced delay), the master's effects, MIDI and Drummer regions as notes (Drummer's stored performance; regions start, trim and nest as GarageBand shows them, the sustain pedal folds into note lengths), audio regions as clips, tempo, time signature, key and cycle come across. Tracks without regions are left out unless `--all-tracks`; `--copy-media` copies audio files next to the job; the job's `import` object keeps what wasn't converted. Audio regions keep their trims and loops (`start`, `length` and `repeat` on the clip, a partial last pass as one more clip; an Apple Loop's pass lasts its beats at the song's tempo), checked against GarageBand's bounce of a trimmed, looped Apple Loop. MIDI regions keep their loops (each pass repeated over the looped span, a partial last pass cut), left and right trims, Transpose (GarageBand gives a MIDI file's region on a bass +12) and Time Quantize: every value in GarageBand's menu, read from a project saved with each one (straight and triplet grids from 1/1 to 1/192, 1/16 and 1/8 Swing A-F with the off-beat at 50 to 71 % of the pair, 5-, 7- and 9-tuplets, and the three mixed grids), the grid running from the region's start, and notes their exact positions (a quantized region stores each note on its grid plus an offset). Tempo changes and ramps come across as the tempo map (GarageBand stores a drawn ramp as a tempo event every half beat; a bounce of a step and a 100 to 140 BPM ramp lands every beat within 2 ms). Each track's volume and pan automation comes across, and the master's volume automation (Mix > Fade Out) as the master's gain curve: GarageBand keeps it as controller curves (volume on the fader's own scale, pan 0-128) and moves straight between its points on those scales, so the gain curve gets points in between; pans use GarageBand's balance law. Apple Loop regions play the way GarageBand plays them: moved into the project key tonic to tonic (the nearest way, a tritone down) whatever either one's mode, with the region's gain (GarageBand sets one per loop), Transpose and Reverse Playback. A test project's bounce checked them: every loop at the bounce's pitch, the volume curve within 0.1 dB, the master fade within 0.3 dB down to -57 dB, pans within 1.5 dB. A send's level automation becomes the send's curve (parameter 28 + its slot, on the fader's scale). Smart Control automation plays where the knob moves a re-created instrument's parameter that builtin:synth has: GarageBand keeps each channel's knob map (knob, plug-in, parameter, range in the parameter's steps, response graph, label) in a keyed archive in the channel strip, and a knob's 0-127 curve goes through it onto Retro Synth's Cutoff, Resonance or Cutoff by Env (a Classic Analog Pad bounce confirmed its Filter knob opens the pad through the filter envelope). Not decoded yet: other plug-in and Smart Control automation, audio region fades, time signature changes, and meters other than 4/4 and 3/4 (read the same way, unchecked). `render song.band` imports and renders: a project's render starts every note within 3 ms of GarageBand's own bounce. The MCP import tool takes `.band` files too.
- builtin:synth `"preset"` and the sampler's `"patch"` take a GarageBand or Logic `.patch` folder by path (from the job's folder), not only a library patch by name, and songs pack the folder with the job.
- builtin:synth additive oscillators: `{"wave": "additive", "partials": [[1, 1], [0.5, 2.76, -0.4]]}` plays a list of sine partials (amplitude, ratio to the note, pan), or a `harmonics` series (count, tilt in dB an octave, odd and even levels), each partial a sine or a saw, square or triangle (`partialWave`), the set moved in Hz by `shiftHz` for inharmonic tones. Partials that would alias drop out at every pitch, glides and vibrato included: harmonic sets play from mip-mapped tables at about a saw's cost, inharmonic ones as a bank of sines. The synth tour's Glass track plays one.
- GarageBand's Vintage Electric Piano, Vintage Clav and Sculpture patches play on builtin:synth by name: the 6 electric piano and 10 Clav patches and 13 of the 15 on Sculpture (`"preset": "Wurlitzer Classic"`, `"Classic D6"`, `"Delicate Bells"`; 550 GarageBand patches there in all with Alchemy's). Their settings (decoded from GarageBand's channel strips and presets, numbered as the instruments' CSParameterOrder lists name them) become a builtin:synth voice and effects: the electric piano's model family (tine with its bell, reed, Electra), decay, release, drive, EQ, Bass Boost, phaser, tremolo and chorus; the Clav's model family, pickup positions, Brilliance, string decay and damping, tone switches, wah, compressor, distortion and modulation effect; Sculpture's exciting object as a class (plucked, struck, bowed, blown, noise), the string's material and losses as its tone and decay, the morph pad, filter, envelopes, vibrato, LFO, envelope and velocity routes, keyboard mode, transpose, waveshaper, Body EQ and delay. A physical model's moving parts (a Wurlitzer's velocity bark, inharmonic strings, key-off sounds, key scaling) are approximated or left out, and the scales are guesses until reference renders. Sculpture patches played by side-chain audio, whose morph envelope is the sound, or made of movement a static voice can't play (Boarding Area, Event Horizon) are refused with the reason, on builtin:synth and in `samples --patch`. 8 of the 29 move into the level window. Every strip's re-creation matches the decoder's proposal value for value.
- DecentSampler instruments play on builtin:sampler without the DecentSampler plugin: `"sampler": {"dspreset": "Malevolence Keys 5"}`, a `.dspreset`, a `.dsbundle` folder or a `.dslibrary` (a zip of a bundle; `"<library>#<preset>"` picks one of several, its samples read from inside it). The preset becomes SFZ regions (as GarageBand's Alchemy patches do): key and velocity ranges, tuning (and the `tunning` spelling), volumes in dB or linear multiplied over the three levels, constant-power pan, start and end, loops (the file's own when the preset gives none) with their crossfade in frames, round robins where every sample at a position plays together, release triggers, tags with their volume and on/off state, choke groups from `silencedByTags`, simple oscillators, and the amplitude envelope on DecentSampler's own curves. Every knob, menu and button fires its bindings at its saved position through the binding's factor and translation (linear, table, fixed value), which is how many presets set their real volume, envelope, effect mix or the one articulation group of several that plays; velocity bindings drive a velocity curve or open a filter per voice. Its effects play after it as built-in effects: filters, EQ, gain, chorus, phaser, convolution, and reverb and delay adding their wet signal to the full dry one. Levels (a sample in the centre plays 5.3 dB under its own level, and DecentSampler's output stage compresses what passes -6 dBFS at 4:1 and stops peaks at 0 dBFS), envelopes, velocity, pan, tuning, loops, round robins, delay and reverb (decay and level, on noise bursts) were matched against DecentSampler 1.11 renders. What is left out (MIDI controller and note bindings, modulators, glide, tag polyphony, buses, the delay's stereo offset) is named in the render's warning; `samples --dspreset <name>` shows a preset's samples, its effects as played and every note. `samples --search` lists the presets (182 here, all of which play), and the folders they play from are no longer listed as kits or multisamples. SFZ round robins now play every region at the chosen `seq_position` too (layered mics and stereo layers used to play one region per note). New check: `scripts/make-test-dspreset.py`.
- More sample libraries found on a Mac: DecentSampler's library folder (its own settings name it; the loose sample packs people keep there), Native Instruments' "<name> Library" folders in /Users/Shared (Maschine expansions' WAV kits, multisamples and loops; Kontakt libraries keep their samples in NI's own containers and stay out), and MeldaProduction's free impulse responses (halls, churches, plates, rooms, cabinets and microphones; some are WAVs named ".flac44"/".flac48") for `convolve`.
- GarageBand's Alchemy patches on its sampler get the synth patches' level window too (they measured -49 to -3.5 LUFS on the probe).
- GarageBand's Alchemy patches play by name: the 313 built from virtual-analog, noise and additive sources on builtin:synth (`"preset": "Hazy Plucked Synth"`, 559 GarageBand patches there in all), and the 33 built on Alchemy's sampler with installed samples, on additive sources layered with samples, on spectral sources in Noise mode, or on granular sources whose grains stand still, on the sampler (`"sampler": {"patch": "Celestial Voices"}`, as SFZ regions). Alchemy keeps its settings as preset text in the channel strip; every parameter is read at rest (Perform knobs and XY pads where the patch saved them, through its modulation maps) and the sources, morph / xfade pad, unison, hard sync, filters, AHDSR envelopes, LFOs, mono, glide, level and Alchemy's own effects racks become builtin:synth settings and effects (wavetables as the basic shape their family is named for, tuned filter types and MSEG movement left out, the scales guesses until reference renders). Additive sources play their partials at rest, from drawn partials (Num Partials and the effect units: Harmonic, Pulse/Saw, Saw+Noise, Beating, Stretch, Shift, Noise, Ripples, Spread, Auto Pan, Alchemy 1.x's pitch and pan profiles, Comb, Filter, EQ) or installed analysis data (.aaz files of every version and coding, each partial's level averaged over its loop), as the waves they amount to (a saw or square spectrum, a hard-synced saw, a detuned stack as unison, up to 12 sines) or an additive oscillator; granular sources at rest play their samples (Speed 100% the sample itself, frozen a short crossfaded loop at Position, a slow scan the sample at its own rate). Formant filters play as EQ peaks at the vowel's formants. The 159 built on spectral synthesis (other than its Noise mode), grains that move, an effect unit that isn't decoded, or content that isn't installed, are refused with the reason, on either instrument and in `samples --patch`. Alchemy's own arpeggiator (on in 57 patches) plays as the track's `arp` like the Arpeggiator's: rate, order, octave range and note length; its step sequencer, shuffle and key velocity are left out. `presets arp` lists 122 patches that arpeggiate. An Amp effect an envelope opens plays at its held level, and of several source effects racks only the one carrying the most level plays (Artificial Persona rendered silent, Gold Coins and Finger On The Pulse at -47 LUFS). A filter that belongs to one source filters only that source: the others, with no filter of its kind, skip it (30 patches change, among them Big Square and Rising High Synth Lead, which played muffled through another source's low-pass), and an effects rack Bandpass whose high cut is at 0 leaves it open (Ghost Voices rendered silent).
- Track `arp`: the held notes play as an arpeggio on any instrument, modelled on Logic's and GarageBand's Arpeggiator (`{"rate": "1/16", "order": "updown", "octaves": 2, "gate": 0.8}`): orders up, down, updown, downup, outsidein, played, random and chord with Logic's four variations each, octaves or inversions, a rhythm grid (`steps`: velocities, rests, ties, chord steps), swing, velocity base/range/random, note length and its randomness, and cycle length. Phrases start and end as Logic's do: back-to-back chords restart the pattern, overlapping ones carry on. New example `examples/arp-tour.json`.
- GarageBand's Arpeggiator plays: the 65 patches that arpeggiate (59 of them on the synths builtin:synth re-creates, such as Classic Analog Arp and Funk Bot) play their arpeggio by themselves, and any track can borrow one, or one of the Arpeggiator's 83 presets, by name (`"arp": "Rolling 8ths"`). Their settings (rate, order and variation, octave range, grid, note length, swing, velocity, cycle) and rhythm grids (a binary property list inside the settings, read by a new portable reader) were decoded from GarageBand's channel strips and presets; the C++ arpeggiator plays the same notes as the decoder's reference on every patch but the one that snaps to a scale (random orders aside). `wavelength presets arp` lists them; `samples --patch` shows a patch's arpeggio as an `arp` object. Latch, keyboard split, scale snapping and (de-)crescendo (used by none or one of GarageBand's settings) are left out with a warning.
- `phaser` effect: first-order all-pass stages swept between `floor` and `ceiling` by a sine LFO (the right channel `spread` of a cycle on), with `feedback` and `mix`. `gate` gains a noise-gate mode: `threshold` (dB) opens it on its own input's level, with `hysteresis`, `hold`, `attack`, `release`, `depth` and `lookahead`. GarageBand patches' Noise Gate and Phaser now play as these.
- Re-created GarageBand synth patches stay near each other's level. Each one plays a probe (C3 held, a C minor chord, effects included) once per process, and one that lands more than 8 dB from the re-creations' typical -25 LUFS moves to that window after its effects (by at most +24 dB). Funk Bot and Velo Techno Bass measured -2 and -3 LUFS from a resonance near self-oscillation; four patches whose filters wait on unmapped modulation measured -58 to -67. 52 of 221 move; the render's warning says how far. `samples --patch --json` shows the builtin:synth patch a synth patch becomes.
- GarageBand's ES2, ES1, EFM1 and Vintage B3 patches play on builtin:synth by name too (107 more; 221 in all): ES2's three oscillators through its mix triangle, FM, hard sync, filter, router routes, envelopes and effects (Digiwaves as sines); ES1 and EFM1 whole; Vintage B3's upper-manual drawbars, percussion, click, vibrato, EQ, distortion, rotor and reverb. Each instrument's own volume sets the level relative to its factory patches' typical volume, so a patch that turns the instrument down for its distortion or EQ keeps that gain structure.
- Patches play more of their own effects: Stereo Delay, Delay Designer, Space Designer (`convolve` with the patch's room), SilverVerb, PlatinumVerb, ChromaVerb, Overdrive, Clip Distortion, Bitcrusher, Chorus, Ensemble, Flanger, Tremolo, Limiter, Gain and Enveloper's level join Channel EQ, Compressor and Tape Delay, in the channel strip's insert order. Effects switched off in the patch (a byte of each plug-in record; 124 Channel EQs, 47 compressors and 81 tape delays in GarageBand's patches, often behind a Smart Control knob) and MIDI effects stay out; a patch effect that can't play here (a room that isn't installed) is left out with a warning instead of failing the render.
- GarageBand's Retro Synth patches play on builtin:synth by name: `"plugin": "builtin:synth", "preset": "Sweet Cream Synth Lead"`. Their saved settings (Retro Synth's 96 parameters, numbered as Logic's CSParameterOrder list names them, decoded from GarageBand's 114 Retro Synth channel strips) become a builtin:synth patch: Analog and Sync oscillators (pulse width, hard sync, sine level), FM as a sine carrier and modulator, Table mode with saws in place of Apple's wavetables, the filter, envelopes, LFO and vibrato, glide, autobend, unison, its chorus or flanger, and the patch's own EQ, compressor and delay. An approximation until reference renders calibrate the cutoff, envelope depth and FM scales; the render warns that it's a re-creation. `presets builtin:synth` lists them under GarageBand; `samples --patch` points synth patches there, and the sampler's error for one names the preset to use.
- GarageBand and Logic patches play nine more of their effects, which about 75 patches left out: AutoFilter (its mode, cutoff, resonance, distortion, dry and output, and its LFO on the cutoff, free or tempo-synced, as an effect `lfo`; the percent scales and LFO waves are guesses, its level-started envelope is left out), Vintage Tube EQ (its program and mid-range boosts and cuts as shelves and peaks, the dB per step guessed, its drive and volume), Echo (its note value read from its presets' names: dotted, straight and triplet from 1/2 down; repeat, dry, wet; its Color as a low-pass, a guess), Multipressor (2 to 4 bands as `multiband`, the top bands playing when fewer are on, each band's compressor, bypass and make-up, the master gain), Direction Mixer (spread as `width`, direction as a pan), Distortion and Distortion II (tanh drive, tone), Exciter (as a high shelf) and Microphaser (as `phaser`). Settings saved at zero (a Smart Control knob raises them) are left out with a note.
- `import song.band` plays tracks on third-party Audio Unit instruments (macOS): the channel strip names the unit (manufacturer, type and subtype codes) and keeps its state as the unit's ClassInfo property list, so the track plays that Audio Unit (`"plugin": "aumu:<subtype>:<manufacturer>"`) with the state written as `patches/<track>.aupreset`, and a built-in stand-in as its `fallback` where the unit isn't installed. Checked on a project saved with Full Bucket Music's MonoFury. These tracks used to be left out. A strip's third-party Audio Unit effects play too (after its own effects, with their saved state), marked with the new plugin-effect key `"optional": true`: an effect plugin that isn't installed or won't load is then left out with a warning instead of failing the render.
- `import song.band` plays GarageBand's transposition track: from each point on, notes on MIDI tracks (not drum tracks) and Apple Loops with a key move by its semitones, judged at each note's start the way GarageBand plays it (a bounce of a looped region crossing a +3 point: the pass before it stayed on C4, the passes after it played E-flat 4).
- `import song.band` brings GarageBand's arrangement track (Intro, Verse, Chorus...) across as the job's `markers`, so the render's report gives loudness per section: each marker's start and name, read from a test project saved with three of them.
- `{"type": "patch", "patch": "Echo Studio"}` plays a GarageBand or Logic patch's effect chain in any track's, bus's or the master's fx: the 177 effect patches in GarageBand's Audio, Aux and Output folders by name (or `"Output/Pop"`), any `.patch` folder by path. `samples --search fxpatch` lists them and `samples --patch` shows one as an effect chain (it used to say its samples weren't installed); songs pack a patch folder used this way.
- `delay` sides and taps: `right` gives the right side its own time and feedback and `crossfeed` sends each side's echoes into the other's line (Logic's Stereo Delay), and `taps` makes a multi-tap delay of up to 32 echoes, each with its time, level, pan and filters, one of them feeding back (`feedbackTap`; Logic's Delay Designer). GarageBand and Logic patches play both sides of their Stereo Delay (13 had sides apart, played at the left time) and every tap of their Delay Designer (6 played one tap; a single centred tap still plays as before); tap pitch shifting is left out with a note.
- builtin:synth key scaling: an oscillator's `keytrack` (dB per octave away from its `keycenter`, C4 unless set) fades it on one side of the keyboard and never raises it above its level, so a tone can change across the keys (a bark on the low notes, a bright top); `amp.keytrack` halves the amp decay and release every octave above C4 (doubles them below) at 1, as a piano's low notes ring longer.
- builtin:synth oscillators: `"filter": false` joins after the filter, `"sync": true` hard-syncs to the first oscillator, and a patch takes up to 12 oscillators (was 6).
- A folder of samples named by their notes plays as a multisample (`{"multisample": "Huge Saw"}`): each file from its note, keys shared with its neighbours, files on one note as round robins, the files' own loop points holding long notes. Libraries name octaves two ways (C4 or C3 = 60), so a few samples' measured pitch decides. `samples` lists such folders.
- GarageBand's sample folders on a Mac: its Alchemy samples (about 50 multisamples: analog and digital synths, basses, pads, organs, solo and synth vocals) and its Ultrabeat drum machine kits (Boutique 808, Boutique 78, the ten Drum Machine Designer kits and six more). Kit names now recognise drum machine abbreviations with take numbers (`BD1`, `SD11`, `HH2`, `HHo`) and `clshat`/`ophat`; they took free keys from 60 before.
- `convolve` effect: a real room, plate or hall from an impulse response, `{"type": "convolve", "ir": "2.0s_Club", "mix": 1}`, with `predelay`, `length`, `highpass`, `lowpass`, `width`, `gain` and an automatable `mix`. Each IR channel is scaled to unit energy, so the wet signal arrives at the dry level. `samples` lists impulse responses (kind `ir`): on a Mac, the 66 Space Designer rooms GarageBand and Logic install, and any audio file in `/Library/Audio/Impulse Responses`, `~/Library/Audio/Impulse Responses` or `$WAVELENGTH_IR_PATH`. New example `examples/convolve-tour.json` with a small synthetic room.
- GarageBand and Logic patches play by name: `"sampler": {"patch": "French Horns"}`. A patch's channels on Logic's samplers play their instruments (Sampler patches carry the whole EXS instrument inside the channel strip; EXS24 and Drum Kit Designer patches name an installed one), merged when there are several (a multi-channel drum kit plays its kit). Ultrabeat drum machine patches play their kit's samples (GarageBand installs each kit as a sample folder), with a warning that Ultrabeat's synthesis is left out. Patches on GarageBand's own synths (Alchemy, Retro Synth, ES2, Sculpture, the Vintage keyboards) are refused by name, and a patch whose samples aren't downloaded says to get GarageBand's Sound Library. `samples` lists the patches that play (37 here with GarageBand's essentials; most of its 1,159 instrument patches are synths or need the Sound Library), and `samples --patch <name>` shows one: channels, instruments, installed samples, effects, and its sends with the Space Designer room each one reaches, ready for a `convolve` bus. A patch plays through its own Channel EQ, Single Band EQ, Compressor and Tape Delay as built-in effects (`"effects": false` for the dry instrument), from their saved settings blocks ("TSPP": a float per parameter, numbered as Logic's CSParameterOrder plists name them), decoded from GarageBand's 1,942 channel strips and Logic's presets.
- EXS instruments play with their own parameters: volume, tuning, transposition, velocity sensitivity and the amplitude envelope (Logic's 0-127 times on their fourth-power curve to 10 s). They used to play with the sampler's default 2 ms attack and 0.25 s release; a track's `attack` or `release` still replaces the instrument's.
- Apple Loops (macOS): the 1,700-odd loops GarageBand and Logic install play as clips by name, `{"file": "lib:Apple Loops/Early Days Piano.caf"}`, and follow the song's tempo by themselves (their tags give their beat count; `bpm` or `speed` override it). `"key": "song"` moves a tonal loop into the job's key at the clip (from `keys`; or a key name), tonic to tonic or to the relative key when one is minor and the other major, within 6 semitones; drum and effect loops play as recorded. `wavelength loops [--search] [--key] [--midi]` lists them with category, key, tempo and length; software-instrument loops carry their notes, which `loops --notes <name> [--key]` prints in beats (a Drummer loop's notes, a slice of a longer performance, moved back to start at beat 0: its slice begins at the bar of its first note) and `import <loop.caf>` turns into a job (a Drummer loop's notes moved the same way). `samples` lists them as the `Apple Loops` library.

- `wavelength stage <job.json>`: gain staging in one command. One render (no stems, picture or deliveries; tracks cached for the next render) measures every track before its fader, and its gain becomes target - stem LUFS. The target comes from the track's role, a word of its name (Kick -12, Clap -17.5, Hats -20, Bass -15.5, Sub -19, Lead -16, Layer -21, Arp -21, Pad -22, Keys -18 ...), then `targets.json` beside the job and `--targets` (a number, `{"lufs", "peak"}` for a peak cap, `role:<Role>` and `bus:<Name>` keys). The gains go to `gains.json` beside the job (what a generator script reads) and with `--apply` into the job; `--report` measures an existing render instead. Tracks no role fits and tracks that peak over 0 dBFS after their fader are listed. Replaces `scripts/stage-gains.py` in the skill and the guide.
- A mix check for a thin bass: when the bass parts (named bass, sub or reese, or written mostly below C3) together put under 15% of their energy below 120 Hz, the report warns and says how to fix it (a sine sub on the same notes, or a bass with more low end). Loudness-based faders can't see a bass that lives in its mids: on 16 trance basses full ones measured 44-77%, and one a listener heard as weak, 3%. Each track's share is in the report as `tracks[].lowShare`.
- `serve`: a live loop in the editor (Live, or V). The selected bars loop seamlessly through Web Audio while you change a mixer (fader up or down from the song's level, pan, mute, solo) and edit notes; each change asks for the loop as it should sound now (`render --loop --cache --mix`, one at a time, the newest request wins) and it crossfades in at the playhead. On Hall of the Mountain Kick's 8-bar drop: a fader or solo is heard about 1 s after the change, a moved bass note about 1.6 s (that track renders again, the other 20 come from the cache). Note edits save to edits.json as you make them; the mixer changes nothing until Send mix to agent leaves the changes as a comment. A job the agent rewrites plays on the next pass. API: `POST /api/loop`, `GET /api/loop`, `GET /api/loop/audio`, `POST /api/loop/stop`.
- `render --mix '{"Bass": {"gain": -3, "pan": 0.2, "mute": true}}'` (or `@file.json`): tracks' faders, pans and mutes for one render, the job file untouched; `--no-png` skips the picture a job asks for.
- `render --cache`: each track's audio (after its effects, before its fader) is kept between renders and reused while nothing that shapes it has changed: its notes (edits.json included), sound, effects and instrument automation, the files it names (size and date), the tracks it follows (sidechain audio, duck and gate triggers), the tempo, the window and this binary. A mixer change renders no track again; a note change renders that one track. On Hall of the Mountain Kick's 8-bar drop: 5.7 s without it, 0.8 s with every track reused, 1.8 s after moving one bass note. The report marks reused tracks `"cached": true`. Least recently used entries go past `$WAVELENGTH_TRACK_CACHE_GB` (default 4).
- `wavelength purge [folder...] [--dry-run] [--json]`: frees the disk renders take while every song stays playable. In each render folder (one where a render wrote report.json: `out/`, a shootout's out folders, the review page's previews) it deletes mix.wav, the stems, FLAC and WAV deliveries and the preview cache; a render whose folder has no MP3 of that render gets mix.mp3 first (an older render's mix.mp3 is replaced, since it would play the wrong mix). Renders written in the last 5 minutes are skipped, and outputs of `wavelength master` (finished masters) stay unless `--masters`. A read-only render folder is left as it is. The report records what went (`purged`). `serve` renders a preview again when purge took its files.
- `wavelength card <plugin> [<preset>...] [+ <plugin> ...]`: patch cards, a picture of how a preset sounds for agents that read images. Every patch plays one probe (a held C4, C2 C3 C4 C5, a C minor chord, eight 16ths) in a child render; one patch draws a full card (the held note's spectrum with a line at every C and the sounding note's harmonics, level, attack and tail, pitch over time, waveform, the four octaves, the chord and the run), several draw a contact sheet to compare. Measurements and flags per patch: the note C4 really sounds and the transpose that fixes it (read from the harmonic spacing when a detuned patch has no single period), slow attack, long tail, percussive, silent keys, moving pitch, sound before the first note, a blurred run. A preset that can't load fails its own card, not the sheet. `--json` has the numbers; MCP tool `patch_card` returns the picture.

### Changed
- GarageBand's Alchemy patches play their note-tuned filters on builtin:synth instead of leaving them out (107 patches; 104 of the 113 that skipped one): Alchemy's filter types 27, 28 and 29 are combs with bipolar, negative and positive feedback, 31 FM, 32 formant, 34 notch, 35 peaking, 36 ring modulation and 38 a downsampler, identified from the names patches give the Perform knobs that move them (Comb Polarity, FM Feedback, Formant Shift, Peak Filter Gain, Ring Mod Mix, Downsample Mix) and from GarageBand's bounces of 16 such patches. They play per voice in a chain with the main filter, in Alchemy's order (a source's own filters, then the main ones, F2 beside F1 or after it as the patch routes it), the sources a source filter doesn't filter joining at the main filters. Their frequency is Alchemy's ten-octave scale from C0 (0.4 with full key follow is the played note), measured within 0.1 % on ring and FM carriers, and a modulation depth that a Perform knob moves moves half as far on them (measured on two bounces; other targets keep the old reading); the knobs' other scales are guesses the notes name. Type 32 used to play as distortion and 38 as a tuned filter left out. A filter that reads as closed at middle C anywhere in the chain still refuses the patch.
- GarageBand's Vintage Electric Piano plays with the key response of GarageBand's own: fitted to a bounce of Classic Electric Piano and Wurlitzer Classic at C3, C4 and C5, the pickup's bark (the measured harmonics) leads the low keys and fades going up, the body grows brighter and louder up to F#4, and low keys ring longer. Classic Electric Piano's C3 used to sound mostly fundamental where GarageBand's is mostly 2nd harmonic (spectral centroid 175 Hz against 362; now 369), and the Wurlitzer's level fell 12 dB from C3 to C5 against GarageBand's; both now land within 2 dB a note. Patch loudness is unchanged.
- GarageBand's Retro Synth patches are calibrated against a GarageBand bounce of eight of them (C3, C4 and C5 each): decay and release times are the time to fall 20 dB, so they play twice as long as before (the bounce's release tails fall 20 dB in 0.8 to 1.0 times the Release time); the filter envelope's full depth is 8.5 octaves (was 10) and the cutoff a quarter octave higher (24 Hz to 24 kHz), which brings the bounce's cutoff tracks within 0.13 octave on average on the five patches without Filter FM (were 0.40); unison voices start in phase and add up (Double plays 3 dB over one voice, four voices 6 dB), detuned 40 x^1.5 cents (was 100 x); oscillators restart at each note with their fundamentals in phase (new oscillator `phase`). Relative levels land within 1.1 dB on four of those five (were up to 7.7 dB off; Way To Live Lead stays 5 dB loud), and Retro Synth patches move to the level window only when more than 12 dB from their typical -20.5 LUFS. Filter FM, the poly glide and the LFO's depth are not calibrated yet.
- `serve` editor: moving a group of notes with the arrow keys previews only its first note (the earliest; the lowest when several start together) instead of playing every selected note on each key press. A single note still sounds as it moves, and "Hear these notes" plays the whole selection. `WLEditor.select(b0, b1, tracks)` also selects those tracks' notes in the bars.
- `serve`: clicking a track name in the song player no longer solos it; soloing lives in the editor (the S button or a right-click).

- Many Wavelength commands started at once (parallel agents, scripts) no longer scan every plugin at the same time, which could exhaust memory. The plugin cache is replaced atomically instead of rewritten in place (a reader could catch it half written and rescan everything), only one process scans at a time while the others wait for its results, and a command that finds everything cached no longer writes the cache. The NKS index and audition indexes are written the same way.
- The plugin scan finds VST3 bundles installed into a VST 2 folder, and no longer looks inside other formats' bundles. A plugin that ends the scan process while loading (V-Pan) gets an error that says so instead of a JSON parse error.

Comments pinned to bars, tracks and notes go to each song's `review.json` for the agent, which answers there. Live updates as the agent writes and renders. `--ui DIR` serves the UI from a folder while working on it.

Beats stay song beats (plugins see the song's position and tempo); notes already sounding start at the window, clips that began earlier play from where it starts. The report's `window` gives the bars and where the files sit in the song.

The source tracks render first; a track that plays rendered clips can't be a source, so it can't recurse.

Curves nobody hears warn: every point outside the parameter's range (it holds still), or movement only where nothing sounds through the effect.

- Job-level `"chords"` (`[[beat, "C#m"], ...]` or `{"bar", "chord"}` entries) and curves that follow them: `{"follow": "root" | "third" | "fifth" | "seventh", "octave": 4}` (or `"from": "A2"`, `"transpose"`, `"as": "note" | "hz" | "midi"`) becomes a switch curve through the chord tones for any automatable frequency or pitch: a built-in cutoff, a plugin parameter (as display text through the plugin) or a semitone pitch. Without `chords`, the chords `lint --harmony` reads from the notes. `examples/automation-tour.json` shows it.
- Automation curves take display text and note names: `[[0, "C#4"], [32, "880 Hz", "switch"]]` on a plugin parameter (`automate` on a plugin effect, `automation.params`) is read by the plugin's own text-to-value once per distinct text before the render, like static `params`; note names reach it as Hz. `"scale": "display"` reads plain numbers as display values. Built-in settings read note names and `Hz`/`kHz`/`dB`/`%` text in curves and as static values (`"cutoff": "A3"`).
- `"switch"` automation points and curves: `[64, 1, "switch"]` holds the previous value until beat 64, then moves to the new one over a short ramp (5 ms, or `"ramp": ms` on the curve object; `"curve": "switch"` makes every point one). On/off switching takes one point per switch instead of a `[t, prev], [t + 0.012, new]` pair.
- Track `"harmony": false` leaves unpitched material out of `wavelength lint` (harmony and voice leading). Lint also skips, on its own, `builtin:sampler` tracks playing one sample with no pitch (noise, or a drum hit under 0.4 s), and lists every skipped track with the reason: a pitched snare roll no longer reads as a Cdim key excursion and a looped noise sweep as a C4 clashing with the pad.
- `analyze` reports `spectrum.tonality`, the share of the energy in spectral peaks (tones and chords high, noise low).
- `render --level-from out/report.json`: the master keeps that render's loudness-target and normalize gains instead of targeting again, so `render --tracks "Lead"` plays and measures a part at the level it has in the full mix (before, a loudness target pushed a lone track up to the target).
- `wavelength lint --harmony`: a harmony check on the notes. It finds the key of every stretch of bars (the job's new `"keys"` list, `--key`, or detection over 8-bar windows that tells a real key change from a passing chord), prints a chord chart (`--chords`) and reports one- or two-bar chords outside the key that go straight back (heard as a one-bar key change), clashes (a minor 2nd or 9th held a beat with one note outside the key) and in-key rubs grouped per pair of tracks, which point at a part playing out of step with the chords. Secondary dominants are listed as fine. Keys take modes (major, minor, dorian, phrygian, lydian, mixolydian). `examples/harmony-tour.json` and `scripts/check.sh` hold the rules to it.
- DAWproject import, more of the song: audio clips become `builtin:audio` tracks (warp markers give the file's tempo so clips follow the song, play start trims, looped clips repeat, fades, "repitch" plays tape-style), automation of the instrument plugin's parameters and of the master volume comes across, and from the Bitwig project Filter, EQ-5, Reverb, Delay-2 and Distortion become built-in effects (read from how 745 projects store them; EQ-5 confirmed the EQ band types, which also fixes one EQ+ type). Effects parked at 0% mix (factory drum kits' pad effects) are skipped.
- Automation curves take `"scale": "normalized"`: values 0..1 of a plugin parameter's range.

What the file can't carry (a DAW's own devices and their samples, launcher clips, audio tracks, device automation) is listed. A Bitwig 6 export of 8 tracks and 1808 notes imports in under a second and renders in 26 s.

### Changed
- `wavelength serve` wears the Wavelength brand: the square-wave mark, Anybody, Instrument Sans and JetBrains Mono (built into the binary, no outside requests), the site's navy, cobalt and amber in dark and light, and track lanes in the song player's palette on wavelength.run.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-09-25

Upgrading from 0.1.0: a song with a master `loudness` target mixes a little differently (the target's gain now goes in front of the final clip/limiter, see Changed; `"loudnessGain": "start"` restores the old sound), bus faders staged from `buses[].lufs` need restaging (buses are now measured before their fader), a render that loses a track exits 1 with `"ok": false`, and every command rejects options it doesn't know.

### Added
- Marker `"checks": false` for sections that are meant as they are: their dropouts are reported as `"intended": true` and they get no arrangement warnings (a trailer's false-ending silence, a soft ambient peak).
- `sections[].preMasterLufs`: each section's loudness before the master gain, rides, chain and loudness target, so a ride the master limiter hands back shows where it went.
- Per-note `dyn` curves: `[start, end]` or `[[beats, level], ...]` shape the `velocityTo` controller (BBC SO's Dynamics) across a note, so a held brass chord crescendos with its timbre changing. The album's orchestral builds had to fake crescendos with volume rides.
- `automation.rides` on tracks, buses and the master: dB curves (one, or named ones) added on top of `automation.gain`. Album agents replaced their fader curves when they added section rides (a pad came out 9 dB too loud in the drops); rides are now a separate layer.
- Arrangement checks in the render report: `dropouts` (the song goes 15 dB quiet for 0.75 s or more and then comes back, with bars and file time) and a warning when a Drop/Chorus/Peak/Hook/Final section lands under +2 dB over the section before it; `sections[].change` gives every step. Album listening found both on most tracks (a two-bar hole before Afterburner's final hit, drops 0-2 dB over their builds).
- `analyze --every S`: loudness of every S-second window, labelled with the render's sections.
- AGENTS.md "Arranging: builds, drops and endings": the defaults that fixed the album's drops and endings.
- `clip` effect: a soft clipper (ceiling, knee, drive) to shave transients before the master limiter; album agents built one out of gain, saturate and gain to stop a 909 kick pumping the limiter. `"kneeDb"` sets where the curve starts in dB under the ceiling (`knee`, a fraction of the ceiling's amplitude, confused three agents: 0.5 starts shaping 6 dB down and distorts).
- Track `"stem": false` skips that track's stem file; `render --tracks` sets it on the muted helper tracks, which wrote 2 GB of silent stems on a nearly full disk.
- `wavelength lint <job.json>`: voice leading between melodic tracks (parallel fifths and octaves in similar motion, voice crossings with `--crossings`), with bar and beat. Pairs that move in octaves or fifths most of the time are reported as doublings, not faults. Best with `--tracks` naming the contrapuntal voices (a fugue's subject, answer and bass).
- `analyze --grid BPM [--div 4]`: each onset's song-time beat and its offset in ms from the nearest grid step, plus the mean offset, to check a break or a groove against the grid.
- `tracks[].postFaderPeakDb`, and when the master limiter works hard a warning naming the three tracks whose peaks feed it most (with how far their peaks stand above their loudness): album agents needed extra renders to find that a spiky kick or clap was limiting the whole song.
- A warning when an automation curve starts late and its first value differs from what the song would otherwise start at (the effect's or parameter's static value, 0 dB for gain rides): a curve holds its first value before its first point, which muffled a whole Theme behind a filter curve that began at bar 65.
- `saturate` `"match": true`: level-matched saturation (output RMS equal to the input's), so changing the drive doesn't change the track's loudness.
- `render --tracks "Lead,Bass"`: render only the named tracks, through the song's buses and master; tracks that key their `duck`, `gate` or sidechain render muted. The report lists them in `onlyTracks`.
- Per-note `vibrato` on sampler tracks (depth, rate, delay, rise), layered on top of `bend`; guitar leads needed dozens of hand-written bend points per note. Per-note `bend` or `vibrato` on a plugin track now warns that it only plays on the sampler.
- `analyze --song-time`: `--start`/`--end` in song time; the render's lead-in is read from the report next to the file. Without it, a window on a render with a lead-in prints a note that the times are file times (agents measured the wrong notes after the 1 s lead-in default).
- A warning when a master loudness target adds more than 8 dB before the master chain, which makes every master compressor and the limiter work that much harder than their settings say.
- The loudness range (LRA, EBU Tech 3342, matches ffmpeg's `ebur128`) in the render report (`mix.lra`), `master` (input and output) and `analyze`.
- Labelled per-section loudness: `tracks[].sections` and `buses[].sections` are `[{"name", "lufs"}]` lists; buses get per-section loudness for the first time.
- `skills/wavelength/`: an agent skill that installs the engine (release build, else from source, docs matched to the binary) and runs the whole song workflow.
- Linux and Windows support. Wavelength builds and runs on macOS (universal), Linux (x86_64 and arm64) and Windows (x86_64), with the platforms' standard CLAP and VST3 folders, worker processes, and caches and settings in each platform's usual place.
- Release binaries for all five targets, built on one Mac with `scripts/build-release.sh` (Docker for Linux, llvm-mingw for Windows), each smoke-tested before packaging. The Windows archive carries the licences of the runtime libraries linked into it (`licenses/`).
- The `/wavelength` skill's installer downloads the release build on Linux and Windows (Git Bash) too, and builds from source on Linux when there is none.
- `examples/arrangement-tour.json`, which `scripts/check.sh` renders and checks: rides, `clip`, level-matched `saturate`, vibrato, a skipped stem, a loudness target and a build that earns its drop.
- `wavelength plugins --block <plugin> [--reason TEXT]` and `--unblock`: a blocked plugin (an unlicensed one that opens a registration window on every load, one that keeps crashing) is marked BLOCKED in `plugins`, and render, params, presets and audition refuse it by name.
- `"retries"` job setting (default 2): a track whose plugin crashes in its worker process is rendered again, and the track says so in its warnings. Altitude crashed about 1 render in 5 under load (a race in its own threads); the song now comes out complete.

### Fixed
- `analyze` could read a note a twelfth or an octave low when a waveform repeats exactly only every few cycles: a chip square lead's B5 read as E4 (an oscillator whose edges fall on the sample grid in a 3-cycle pattern). A reading is now moved up to k times the frequency when nearly all the energy sits on every k-th harmonic and the signal is nearly as periodic at the shorter period, which leaves real low notes alone (a cello's C2 is mostly its 3rd harmonic and still reads C2). Checked on 256 synthetic tones, 85 sampled and BBC SO notes, and the stems of five songs.
- An unknown option at the end of a command (`analyze mix.wav --bogus`) was reported as missing its value; it is now named as unknown, with the options the command takes.
- `analyze --grid` printed the mean distance from the grid as "mean offset (+ = late)". It now prints both: the signed mean offset (the groove's push or lay) and the mean distance (`grid.meanOffsetMs` and `meanAbsOffsetMs` in JSON).
- `analyze --every` ignored `--song-time`; with it, windows now start on the first beat and print song time.
- The `clip` distortion warning fired when a tenth of the samples were shaped (it counted both channels against a fifth), and always advised `"kneeDb": 2`, even when the knee already was.
- `analyze` took a lead-in from any report.json next to (or above) a file, so a decoded MP3 in an unrelated folder got a lead-in note. It now uses a report only when it wrote that file (a render's mix or stem, or a `master` output).
- `analyze` gave no pitch for windows shorter than about 85 ms; a 60 ms window (one short note) now reads its pitch (fundamentals down to about 50 Hz).
- `analyze` onset times were about 25 ms early (the spectral frame's start, not the attack); they are now within one hop (about 5 ms), measured on a click track. A window that cuts into a sound no longer reports an onset at its first frame.
- `wavelength master` on a mix rendered with a lead-in put every section 1 s off and added a second lead-in. It now reads the lead-in from the render's report.json (or `--input-lead-in`), lines the markers up, and keeps the lead-in on the output unless `--lead-in` says otherwise.
- Loudness of a window shorter than 0.4 s (an `analyze` window, a one-hit section) read -120 LUFS; it is now the window's plain K-weighted loudness.
- A plugin that opens a window while rendering (a licence or registration dialog) no longer holds the render until the hang timeout: on macOS the worker is killed within about 2 s and the track fails with a message that suggests `plugins --block`.
- Kits with `"variants": "roundrobin"` left the General MIDI alternate keys (35 kick 2, 40 snare 2, 43 floor tom, 48 tom, 57 crash 2...) empty, so a GM drum part went partly silent. They now play their drum's round-robin takes.
- `wavelength master --chain` read a `{"master": {...}, "markers": [...]}` file (a job without tracks) as an empty chain and passed the mix through unchanged without a word. That shape now works, and a chain with no effects and no loudness target is an error naming the keys it found.
- The chorus, delay, reverb and rotary effects could read one sample past their delay buffer when a modulated delay time landed a hair below a whole sample, returning whatever memory came next (the likely cause of a Dexed lead through chorus and delay reading +460 LUFS once).
- A render with failed tracks reports `"ok": false` with an `error` naming them and exits 1. It used to say `"ok": true` with only `failedTracks` showing that the mix was missing a part.
- Garbage samples (not a number, or above +30 dBFS; DUNE 3 once wrote a single +79 dBFS sample into a brass chord) no longer click, excite every reverb downstream and wreck the track's loudness reading: they are muted after every plugin, effect and built-in instrument, the track warns with the time, and a track reading above +6 LUFS warns that its output is broken.

### Changed
- The weak-drop check also covers any section after a build-like one (Build, Rise, Pre..., Ramp, Climb, Lead-in, Ignition), whatever it is called, and asks an escalation (Climax, Peak, Finale after another payoff) to rise at least 0.5 dB. Build -> Discovery and Final Act -> Climax went unchecked.
- A curve that starts late only warns when the track sounds before it (its first note or clip): a melody whose filter curve begins with its first note at bar 21 no longer warns.
- Every command rejects options it doesn't know, naming the ones it takes. They used to be ignored: an agent with newer docs than its binary ran `render --tracks` and silently got the whole song.
- The limiter's warning adds its gain reduction per marker section (average and maximum), so the drops' limiting shows next to the quiet sections'.
- A master `loudness` target adds its gain in front of the chain's trailing run of `clip` and `limiter` effects (`"loudnessGain": "peak"`, the default) instead of before the whole chain, so glue compressors and EQ ahead of it keep their settings and the section contrast (three album tracks lost 1-2 dB of contrast to the old placement) while a `clip` before the limiter still sees the loud signal. `"limiter"` puts it in front of the last limiter only; `"start"` restores 0.1.
- The limiter's gain-reduction warning says how long it worked (share of the time above 3 dB and 6 dB), so one hot transient reads differently from sustained crushing, and it also warns when more than 10 % of the song sits above 6 dB of reduction.
- The automatic `parallel` setting takes the system load into account: with other renders already filling the cores, a render starts fewer workers instead of oversubscribing the CPU (ten agents rendering at once drove the load average past 70 on 8 cores).
- `buses[].lufs` and `buses[].levels` are measured before the bus fader, like a track's, so `gain` = target − lufs works for buses too. They used to be measured after it.
- A plugin library that fails to load names the library and the reason (on Linux the VST3 SDK only said "dlopen failed").

Many plugins also need their own sidechain switch turned on (MTurboComp "Side-chain input (Detector)").
- `builtin:audio`: audio clips on the timeline. Each clip is a WAV placed by `beat` or by `endAt` (it ends exactly on that beat: reverse swells), fitted to the song tempo from its own `bpm` with pitch-preserving stretch (Signalsmith Stretch, MIT) or tape-style (`"stretch": false`), transposed with `pitch`, trimmed (`start`, `length` or `beats`), reversed, with gain and fades. Example `examples/clips-tour.json`.
- Plugin tracks render in worker processes, several at once (`render --jobs N` or `"parallel"` in the job; default half the cores, up to 4; 0 = everything in one process). A track whose plugin crashes or hangs is left out of the mix and listed in the report's `failedTracks` instead of failing the song; job mistakes (unknown preset, bad state) still fail the render. Built-in instruments stay in-process; tracks are mixed as they finish, so memory stays flat.
- `audition --retag` recomputes every index's tags from stored measurements; a new audition run retries earlier failures and drops presets that are no longer listed. Serum 1 `.fxp` patches in Serum 2's folders are no longer listed for Serum 2.
- `wavelength audition <plugin> [--jobs N] [--limit N] [--rebuild]`: renders every preset once (C4 for 1 s) in worker processes, each keeping one plugin instance, and indexes how it sounds in `~/Library/Caches/wavelength/audition/`: octave offset and cents, loudness, brightness, band balance, attack, decay, sustain, release, width, onsets, and whether it plays by itself. A preset that crashes or hangs its worker is recorded and skipped. `presets` then shows tags (octave -1, dark, warm, bright, sub, bassy, pluck, slow attack, rhythmic, long release, wide, self-playing, silent, ...) that `--search` finds, and `--json` includes the measurements.
- `wavelength analyze <file.wav | render-dir> [--start] [--end]`: pitch (YIN: note, cents, confidence), spectral centroid, rolloff and band balance (sub to air), stereo width and correlation, onsets, and envelope (attack, decay, sustain, active time). A render folder analyzes its mix, every stem and every marker section. Agents no longer need their own FFT scripts.
- `scripts/stage-gains.py <song>`: sets each track's fader from the last render's stem LUFS and the song's `targets.json`.
- Orchestral writing: per-note articulations (`"art": "spiccato"` with a track `articulations` map of keyswitch keys) insert the keyswitch just before each change; track `range` warns about notes an instrument can't play; `velocityTo` turns note velocities into a controller curve (`{"param": "Dynamics"}` or `{"cc": 1}`) for libraries whose long notes ignore velocity.
- `multiband` effect: 2-4 bands on Linkwitz-Riley crossovers that sum back flat, each band with its own effect chain, gain, solo and mute (multiband compression, band saturation).
- `master.loudness`: a target in LUFS; the gain into the master chain is found in a few passes so the mastered mix lands on it (`mix.loudnessGainDb` in the report).
- `wavelength master <mix.wav> --chain <chain | job>`: masters a finished mix without re-rendering, with loudness before and after per section; the chain can be a file or inline JSON.
- Personal job defaults: `~/Library/Application Support/Wavelength/defaults.json` (or `$WAVELENGTH_DEFAULTS`) holds top-level job settings, e.g. `{"leadIn": 1}`, that every job gets unless it sets them itself; the report lists them in `defaultsApplied`. Jobs Wavelength builds internally (`wavelength master`) ignore them.
- `leadIn`: seconds of silence before the song in the mix and stems (streaming uploads), and `--lead-in` on `wavelength master`.

### Changed
- Automation points at the same beat keep the order they are written in (`[16, 0], [16, 1]` jumps from 0 to 1); they used to be sorted by value.
- The limiter detects true (inter-sample) peaks by default; mixes limited at the same ceiling come out a few tenths of a dB quieter.
- Kit auto-mapping groups takes of one sound and maps toms by number, so some keys of auto-mapped kits play different files than before (explicit `map` entries are unaffected).

### Fixed
- An option given without its value (`analyze out --end --json`) is an error instead of swallowing the next option.
- Plugin delay compensation: instruments and effects that report latency (lookahead limiters, linear-phase EQs, LA-2A, ...) are rendered that many samples longer and shifted back into place, so their tracks no longer land late against the mix. The report gives `tracks[].latencyCompensatedMs`.
- A failed render no longer leaves the previous render's `mix.wav`, `report.json` and stems behind; it writes `{"ok": false}` to `report.json`.
- `params` set on top of a `state` stick on plugins that apply state late (Surge XT); `params` inspection processes a few blocks first so such plugins show real values.
- Clamp warnings no longer fire for in-range values (float precision) and show the range.
- Limiter warning reads as a positive gain-reduction figure with the time it happened.
- The CLI exits without running plugin static destructors, which crashed some plugins at shutdown after the work was already written.
